## What Is Loss Prevention Software?

**Loss prevention software**

## The Five Sources of Retail Shrinkage (and How Software Addresses Each)

**shrinkage detection**

### 1. External Theft

Traditional: Security guards, EAS systems, camera playback after the fact.

**AI-powered approach:**

**Key capabilities:**

- Concealment detection at self-checkout and staffed registers

- Ticket switching and label tampering alerts

- EAS bypass detection

- Coordinated group behavior recognition

### 2. Employee Theft

Traditional: Tip lines, random audits, and hope.

**AI-powered approach:****POS exception reporting**

- Voids and no-sales after a payment

- Excessive discounts or employee purchases

- Refund transactions without corresponding return merchandise

- Sweethearting — passing items without scanning

- Under-ringing — scanning cheaper items for expensive products

- Coupon stacking and discount abuse

These patterns are invisible in aggregate data but become obvious when AI continuously monitors every transaction against baseline behavior. The system doesn't just flag individual exceptions — it builds behavioral profiles for each cashier, identifying patterns that develop over days or weeks.

**Key capabilities:**

- Cashier behavioral profiling and anomaly scoring

- Void/refund ratio tracking by associate and shift

- Sweethearting detection via video-POS correlation

- Discount and coupon abuse pattern recognition

### 3. Administrative Errors

Traditional: Manual inventory counts that catch errors weeks or months after they happen.

**AI-powered approach:**

**Key capabilities:**

- Automated cycle counts using shelf-scanning cameras

- Real-time inventory discrepancy alerts

- Receiving dock verification (comparing physical deliveries against purchase orders)

- Transfer and replenishment tracking

### 4. Vendor Fraud

Traditional: Checking delivery counts manually — time-consuming and error-prone.

**AI-powered approach:**

**Key capabilities:**

- Automated receiving count verification

- Purchase order vs. physical delivery reconciliation

- Substitution detection (wrong items delivered)

- Invoice manipulation alerts

### 5. Organized Retail Crime (ORC)

Traditional: Reactive — reviewing footage after a series of thefts are reported.

**AI-powered approach:**

**Key capabilities:**

- Cross-store incident correlation

- Behavioral pattern recognition (distraction techniques, lookout identification, escape route mapping)

- Evidence packaging formatted for law enforcement

- Integration with regional ORC intelligence databases (where available)

## Core Features of Modern Loss Prevention Software

**loss prevention software**

### POS Exception Reporting

**POS exception reporting**

- Unusual void/refund ratios per cashier

- Transactions with no corresponding video activity (potential sweethearting)

- Refund amounts that spike during specific shifts

- Coupon or discount abuse patterns

- No-sale drawer openings

- Price override patterns

- Gift card activation anomalies

The best systems don't just flag exceptions — they rank them by severity and financial impact, so LP teams can prioritize investigations. They also build historical profiles for each cashier, so the system can distinguish between a one-time anomaly and a developing pattern.

### Video-Based Shrinkage Detection

**Shrinkage detection**

- Track item movement from shelf to basket to checkout

- Detect concealment behavior (items placed in bags, under clothing)

- Identify ticket-switching and label tampering

- Monitor self-checkout lanes for missed scans and age-restricted item bypasses

- Correlate POS transactions with video evidence automatically

### Self-Checkout Loss Prevention

**Retail theft prevention**

- **Missed scan detection**

- **Age verification bypass**

- **Coupon fraud**

- **Sweethearting**

- **Weight discrepancy alerts**

Self-checkout monitoring typically delivers the fastest ROI of any LP use case, with payback periods of 3-6 months.

### Automated Alert Engine

Configurable rules let you set thresholds for different alert types:

- "Notify LP manager when refund transactions exceed $200 in a single shift"

- "Alert when queue at self-checkout exceeds 3 people for more than 2 minutes"

- "Flag any transaction where the cashier voids more than 5% of items"

- "Notify when high-value items are removed from the shelf but not scanned at checkout"

Alerts should come with attached video clips and transaction details — giving investigators everything they need to act immediately.

### Case Management and Evidence Packaging

When an exception is detected, the system should automatically:

- Save relevant video segments (before, during, and after the incident)

- Link transaction records to video evidence

- Generate a case file with timeline, evidence, and recommended actions

- Support export for law enforcement or legal proceedings

- Track case status from detection through resolution

### Multi-Store Dashboard

For enterprise retailers, a centralized dashboard provides:

- Shrinkage trends across all locations

- Store-level risk scoring

- LP team performance metrics (cases opened, investigations completed, recovery amounts)

- Heat maps of exception types by store, shift, and department

- Benchmarking across stores to identify high-risk locations

## Integration with Other Retail Systems

**Loss prevention software**

### POS System Integration

The foundation of LP technology. The system must ingest every transaction in real time, including:

- Transaction details (items, quantities, prices, discounts)

- Payment method and tender type

- Cashier ID and timestamp

- Void, refund, and return details

- Coupon and promotion applications

### Workforce Management Integration

Connects LP data to scheduling and HR systems:

- Map exception patterns to specific associates and shifts

- Identify staffing patterns that correlate with high shrinkage

- Support performance management and coaching workflows

- Flag associates with unusual exception patterns for investigation

### Inventory Management Integration

Bridges the gap between system inventory and physical reality:

- Compare AI-detected shelf counts against system inventory

- Identify ghost inventory (system says in-stock, shelf is empty)

- Flag receiving discrepancies before they compound

- Support cycle count workflows with AI-generated counts

### Communication and Workflow Integration

Ensures LP insights reach the right people at the right time:

- Slack, Teams, or SMS alerts for real-time notifications

- Task management integration for corrective actions

- BI platform integration for trend analysis and reporting

- Email digest reports for management review

## How to Reduce Retail Shrinkage: A Practical Framework

**how to reduce retail shrinkage**

### Step 1: Establish Baseline Metrics

Before deploying any new tool, measure your current shrinkage rate. Most retailers discover their actual shrinkage through annual inventory counts — but that's too infrequent. Start tracking:

- Exception rates by store and by shift

- Refund and void ratios per cashier

- Inventory discrepancy rates by department

- Self-checkout exception rates

- Return fraud incidence by store

### Step 2: Deploy POS Exception Reporting First

This delivers the fastest ROI. Most retailers recover 3-5x the cost of the system within the first year by identifying transaction fraud that was previously invisible. Start with:

- Void/refund ratio monitoring

- No-sale drawer opening tracking

- Sweethearting detection (video-POS correlation)

- Cashier behavioral profiling

### Step 3: Layer in Video-Based Detection

Once your POS exception program is running, add video analytics for:

- Self-checkout monitoring (the highest-shrinkage lane type)

- Receiving dock verification

- High-value department surveillance

- Concealment detection at exits

### Step 4: Build a Continuous Improvement Loop

Monthly shrinkage reviews should analyze:

- Which detection rules are generating the most actionable alerts

- Which stores are improving vs. deteriorating

- Whether new exception patterns are emerging

- ROI by use case — double down on what works

- Seasonal adjustments based on historical patterns

### Step 5: Scale and Optimize

As your program matures:

- Expand from pilot stores to full network

- Refine alert thresholds based on actual false positive rates

- Add predictive models to identify emerging risks

- Integrate with workforce management for coaching workflows

- Share best practices across stores based on top performers

## How to Evaluate Loss Prevention Solutions

**retail loss prevention software**

### Accuracy and Performance

- What is your detection accuracy for POS exceptions? (Look for > 90%)

- What is your false positive rate? (Look for < 5%)

- How quickly are alerts delivered? (Look for < 10 seconds for real-time use cases)

- Can you provide case studies from similar retailers?

### Deployment and Infrastructure

- Does it work with my existing cameras? (Camera-agnostic is preferred)

- What's the deployment timeline per store? (Look for 1-2 weeks)

- Do you support edge processing for low latency? (Critical for real-time LP)

- What's the bandwidth requirement? (Edge processing reduces this dramatically)

### Scalability and Integration

- What's your largest retail deployment? (Look for 100+ stores)

- Does it integrate with my POS system? (Verify compatibility)

- Does it integrate with my workforce management system?

- Can it scale from 10 stores to 500 stores without re-architecture?

### Privacy and Compliance

- How do you handle data privacy and GDPR compliance?

- Where is data stored? (On-premise, regional cloud, global cloud?)

- Do you support automatic anonymization?

- What data retention policies are configurable?

### Support and Evolution

- How often do you release new models and capabilities?

- What does your customer support look like? (24/7? Dedicated CSM?)

- How do you handle model updates across a large deployment?

- What's your product roadmap for the next 12-18 months?
